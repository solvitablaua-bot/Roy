# Memory.md

## Instructions

Use Memory.md to document everything that we are doing in this project:

- what we are learning;
- the key takeaways and ideas that we discuss together;
- progress on our project;
- any important decisions.

Keep Memory.md up to date at all times. Every time we start a new session, read `Claude.md` and `Memory.md` to gain full project context.

## What we are learning

- Claude Code runs in a cloud container, not on the iPad. Project files live in the GitHub repository `solvitablaua-bot/Roy` and can be opened on the iPad through the Claude app, the GitHub app or a git app such as Working Copy.
- Claude automatically reads a file called `CLAUDE.md` at the top of the repository when a session starts. Files in subfolders, such as `Roy/Claude.md`, are only read if something points to them.
- Roy can't write yet, so he speaks his instructions and voice dictation turns them into text. Claude has to understand messages with odd spellings and made-up words.
- Sounds (roar, rumble, chimes) are made by code in the browser (Web Audio), so there are no sound files to copy or license. Tops the guide reads lines aloud with the iPad's built-in voice. Sound only starts after the first tap, which is a browser rule.
- Claude tests the game in a Chrome-based test browser, but Roy plays on an iPad (Safari), which can draw some layouts differently. A picture that works out its own size from its shape can go wrong on the iPad. It is safer to put pictures in boxes with a fixed size, so the picture just fills the box. When Roy says something looks broken, believe him even if it looked fine in testing.

## Key takeaways and ideas

- The game is "Roy's Dino Park": Roy is the park creator who hatches, names, cares for and houses dinosaurs.
- Roy is also a co-designer. His own ideas get added to the game over time.
- It should feel like the Jurassic movies (jungle, amber, park gate, warning signs, roars) but stay exciting and never scary.
- Gamification: stars, ranger levels, badges, a daily mystery egg, and no way to lose.
- Every build step should end with something Roy can play, so he sees his game grow.
- Hidden secrets make exploring fun: tapping the volcano, the moon and the bush each gives a star, and finding all three gives the Jungle Explorer badge. More secrets can be added in each new area.
- Roy's first big idea (spoken): "When I tap on that egg, it should hatch and a baby raptor Blue would come out." This became the Egg Lab.
- Tapping the egg 3 times (crack, crack, hatch) is more exciting for Roy than hatching on the first tap.
- Roy's second idea (spoken): "Jurassic World should be on an island surrounded by water. To get to the island, you have to take a boat and then go through the jungle. Then you can go through the gates." This became the trip at the start of the game.
- Roy thinks about the whole world of the game, not just the dinosaurs. His ideas are about journeys and places.
- Roy's third idea (spoken, not finished yet, he paused): "Now we're building the park and in the park there's gonna be 20 cages with dinosaurs. The cages are gonna be big and far away from each other." Then he added: "It's gonna be Triceratops, Spinosaurus, and Dilophosaurus." So the first 7 are Blue the raptor, T-Rex, Brachiosaurus, Compsognathus, Triceratops, Spinosaurus and Dilophosaurus. Then Roy asked: "Can AI find just some other dinosaurs and put them in the rest?" So Claude picked the other 13.
- Roy is happy to let Claude choose when he runs out of ideas. Claude picked well-known dinosaurs that look different from each other, so each cage is easy to tell apart.
- Roy's fourth idea (spoken): "Dilophosaurus needs meat every day. Brachiosaurus needs to be fed leaves every day. Ankylosaurus, figure out what kind of food he wants to eat. T-Rex needs meat every day. And the dinosaurs need to be washed, scrubbed in a bath in their cages so they can be clean." This became Dino Care.
- A daily routine (feed and wash every day) gives Roy a reason to come back each day, like having real pets.
- Choosing the right food is a gentle guessing game that teaches what each dinosaur ate.
- Roy's fifth idea (spoken): "I want the people to go to the jungle and find all kinds of new species of dinosaurs. And when they find a new species, we put it in the cage. That will be the ranger adventure." This became Ranger Missions.
- Roy's ideas now connect into one loop: go on a mission, find a new species, it gets a new cage in the park, then feed and wash it every day.
- Roy's sixth idea (spoken): "In the Dino Book, collect all the missions. Think of at least 10 missions that are described in the Dino Book, and you can go through the book and choose which mission you want to complete and what's good in that mission." This turned the Dino Book into a book of missions.
- Roy likes choosing for himself: the book lets him pick any mission in any order, and each page shows what he can win.
- Mystery silhouettes (a dark shadow of the dinosaur until it is found) make Roy curious about who is hiding.
- Roy's seventh idea (spoken): "You need to create a pet shop where there's going to be pet dinosaurs and people can come buy their own pet dinosaur." This became the Pet Shop, where Roy is the shopkeeper.
- Listening to what each customer wants and finding the matching pet is a gentle matching game that practises noticing features (horns, crests, long necks, colours, what they eat).
- Roy's feedback on the Pet Shop (spoken): "I want to see the people who are coming inside the pet shop. I want to see their faces and their whole body." Roy cares about the people in his world, not only the dinosaurs, and wants them big and detailed.
- Roy's eighth idea (spoken): "Now from the Dino Book, build all the missions that are described in the Dino Book." Roy noticed the missions all played the same way (tap the bushes). Each mission should do what its page says.
- Different kinds of play keep missions fresh for Roy: following footprints, listening for a sound, watching for someone peeking, hopping across stones, and spotting the odd one out.
- Roy's ninth idea (spoken): "I want a memory game of dinosaurs in my park. When you tap on cards, dinosaurs pop out. You have to find two of the same dinosaurs." This became the Memory game.
- Choosing a small, middle or big game lets Roy pick how hard it is, and the bigger game gives more stars.
- Roy's tenth idea (spoken): "When you get to the park gates, you have to run as fast as you can so you don't get caught by a pack of raptors that are waiting for you by the park gates." This became the Raptor Run.
- Roy likes a bit of thrill and danger. The chase keeps it exciting but never scary: the raptors are friendly cartoon colours, they never catch you, and they end up bonking into the door with dizzy stars.
- Roy's eleventh idea (spoken, the end was cut off): "When you finish running from the raptors, when the door opens, I want a T-Rex roar to..." Built as a T-Rex appearing in the big gate with a huge roar. Still to ask Roy what the roar should do (for example scare the raptors away).
- Roy's twelfth idea (spoken): "For my park, where all of the cages are, I want also a mystery dinosaur in one of the cages." This became the Mystery Cage, and it also delivers the "daily mystery" idea from the first plan.
- Guessing from clues (a sound, then a hint) before the big reveal turns each mystery into a little quiz.
- Roy's thirteenth idea (spoken): "I also want another mystery dinosaur for my park." This became a second Mystery Cage.
- Roy's fourteenth idea (spoken): "I want 10 more mystery dinosaurs for my park." This became 10 more Mystery Cages (12 in all) and 10 more secret dinosaurs (18 in all).
- Roy loves the mystery reveal. He keeps asking for more of it, so mystery and surprise are a strong theme for future ideas.
- Roy's fifteenth idea (spoken): "I want the last mystery dinosaur's skin to be made out of gold." This became the golden dinosaur in Mystery Cage 12.
- Roy's sixteenth idea (spoken): "When you guess the mystery dinosaur that has the golden skin, there is gonna come a golden monster made out of gold." This became the Golden Monster.
- A "monster" for Roy should be big and exciting but friendly: the Golden Monster smiles, waves and gives treasure.
- Roy's seventeenth idea (spoken): "If you don't make it to the park gates, I want the raptors to catch you and eat you." Roy wants real stakes in the chase, so tapping fast actually matters.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want the new species in the dinosaur adventures. The new species are not alive. They're dead. They're bones. The new species are bones." This turned the missions into fossil digs: the rangers dig up skeletons, like real palaeontologists.
- Digging (brushing away sand with a finger) is the same rubbing action as washing a dinosaur, so Roy already knows how to do it.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "When the boat is going to the island, I want a Mosasaurus coming and attacking the ship." This became the Mosasaurus attack on the boat trip.
- Roy keeps adding danger to the journey (raptors at the gate, a sea monster on the boat). Each danger is something Roy can beat by tapping, so he feels like the hero.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "When the Mosasaurus attacks the ship, I want the ship to sink." Like the raptors, Roy wants real stakes: if he is too slow, the boat sinks. This matches what he asked for in the Raptor Run.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "You can also make the dinosaur come out of Jurassic World, out of the game. The dinosaur comes out of the game and comes into the house." This became Dino Escape.
- Roy likes the line between the game and the real world: a dinosaur that "escapes" the screen feels magical. A real camera view (the dinosaur in Roy's own room) is not possible in the private preview, so the house is a cartoon living room for now. If the game is later hosted on its own website, a camera version could be an idea, but only with a parent's say-so.
- Roy's idea (spoken, said twice to be clear): "When someone is playing this game, you can press a button and the dinosaur just comes out of the game you're playing and comes in the house where the real person is living." Roy really means the player's real home, not a cartoon house, so the dinosaur now appears in the room through the iPad camera.
- Roy said it a third time (spoken): "When you are playing this game, I want the dinosaur to come out of the screen, where the person is playing the game." "Where the person is" could mean the player themselves, so a button now turns the camera round to show the player with the dinosaur.
- Roy said it more times (spoken): "I want the dinosaurs to come out of the screen. Not come out from the not-real screen. It has to come out for real... like anywhere. I want the danger to come out of the screen for real." The cartoon house and the glass inside the game felt "fake" to Roy, because they were a screen inside the screen. What he wants is for the iPad's own screen to look like it breaks, with the dinosaur bursting out at the player, at any time.
- Roy understood that a real dinosaur can't come out of an iPad. Claude told him so gently and offered the nearest things: the camera view and a printable paper dinosaur (not built yet; waiting for Roy's answer).
- Roy finally explained it fully (spoken): "I like what you built, but that's not the thing I asked. You can aim where you want the dinosaur to come out in the room, and then you press a button, then it shoots out, and then it appears somewhere in the room." So his idea all along was a camera game: aim at your real room, press a button, and the dinosaur flies out of the iPad and lands there.
- Lesson: when Roy repeats an idea many times, the build has missed something. Asking him to describe exactly what he does and sees (what he taps, what happens next) would have found this sooner.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "When the dinosaurs break out of the screen, you have to shoot them with a gun so they can fall asleep and go back inside the screen." Like the rangers in the films, who use sleepy (tranquilliser) darts. Built as a sleepy-dart gun: the dinosaurs are never hurt, they just fall asleep.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want the Golden Monster not to be good. I want it to be bad." Roy wants a villain to beat. The Golden Monster is now a greedy, grumpy baddie who wants to steal the golden dinosaur, and Roy is the hero who stops him.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "You have to guess where the two same dinosaurs are, and if you guess the two of the same dinosaurs you'll get a reward, like a new sleepy dart blaster." The memory game already existed, so the new part is the prize: each win gives a new blaster. This links two of Roy's ideas (the memory game and the sleepy darts) into one loop: win at memory, get a better blaster, use it on escaped dinosaurs.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "You're taking a film of the room, the real room, and then suddenly, there, it appears: a hologram." While the camera shows the real room, a glowing hologram dinosaur pops up by surprise, like the hologram dinosaurs in the films' visitor centre. This fits Roy's love of surprises and of dinosaurs showing up in the real world.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want to take a video, and then when I'm taking the video, a hologram appears of a dinosaur." The holograms already appear in the live camera view. What is new is keeping a real video recording, so Roy can watch it again or show it to others. That breaks the current rule that the camera picture is never recorded or saved, so it needs a parent's decision first. Asked the parent; not built yet.
- Roy explained what a hologram is (spoken): "A hologram is like where you are... it's like an animal coming out of the ground, but you can go totally through it, like a ghost." So holograms now rise up out of the floor, and your finger passes right through them like a ghost.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want my mystery dinosaurs to be like ghosts." After hearing about holograms, Roy wanted them in the game itself, not only in the camera view (which he can't see yet). The mystery dinosaurs are now ghost holograms.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want all the dinosaurs in the Jurassic World to be just ghosts. I want the Mosasaurus to be a ghost. I even want the T-Rex to be a ghost. Every dinosaur." This became Ghost World.
- Roy's idea (spoken): "I want, when the dinosaurs break out of the screen, the rainbow blaster." Roy didn't want to wait to win it, so the Mega Rainbow Blaster is now his from the start.
- Empty cages are a good way to keep Roy's ideas coming: each one has a "?" sign, and Tops asks "Roy, which dinosaur should live here?"
- Idea: the game itself could listen to Roy too, for example saying a name out loud to name a new dinosaur.
